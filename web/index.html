Reading the radar

The little house in the middle is home. Towns are marked with a dot and their name; airports with a ringed circle and their three-letter code (like JFK). The solid ring is the distance you picked under Display; dotted rings are in between.

When a plane flies close over the house, a big card shows its name, where it's going, how high it is, and which way to look up. Every half hour or so the screen shows today's highlights.

Plane and helicopter icons point the way they're flying. The dots behind them are their trail. The short line in front shows where they'll be in about a minute.

The sweep line spins like a real radar. Each plane flashes and updates as the line passes over it.

Next to each plane: the flight (e.g. "Delta 1234"), the plane model, and how high it is. Small private planes show their registration (like N172SP) instead of a flight name.

The panel on the right shows the time, how many planes are nearby, and the closest one.

Buttons on the radar

KEY – zoom in (look closer). BOOT – zoom out (look farther).

Both buttons together – switch between the radar and the airport board.

When a big card is showing (a plane overhead, or today's highlights), any button closes it.

Hold BOOT – hide or show the plane labels.

Hold KEY for 2 seconds – show this page's address. Keep holding for 10 seconds to erase everything and start over.

PWR – turn on (one click) or off (hold).

Good to know

The screen has no light of its own, like a paper page. Put it where daylight or a lamp reaches it.

Some planes don't share their position, so a few (often military) won't appear.

Typical heights: airliners cruise at 30,000–40,000 ft, climb and descend near airports, and small planes and helicopters stay below about 10,000 ft.

If the screen says it can't reach your Wi-Fi, join the FlightRadar-Setup network on your phone to pick a network again.
